# HUNTIQ public retailer research — 2026-08-31

## Target: local price and clearance semantics

Public Target Help pages confirm several rules that should shape HUNTIQ ingestion and scoring:

- Pricing, promotions and availability may vary by store and online.
- Target's app must show the **current store location** when an in-store price is used for a price match.
- A lower price from a **different Target store** is excluded from in-store price matching.
- **Online clearance pricing is separate from in-store clearance pricing.** Target states that in-store clearance pricing is not visible online and can only be found/purchased in store.
- Target's `Was` strikethrough price on Target.com/app is based on the **90-day median offered price**, excluding sales, clearance and limited-time promotions.

Engineering implications:

1. Never merge Target online, pickup/delivery, and in-store clearance observations into one raw history stream.
2. Store identity is mandatory for any Target local-price observation.
3. Online absence of a clearance price must not be interpreted as evidence that an in-store clearance price does not exist.
4. Community receipt/photo reports can become useful first-party evidence for in-store clearance, but should be labeled user-submitted until independently confirmed.
5. Target's 90-day median reference supports HUNTIQ's use of robust medians instead of naive averages, while remaining only retailer-provided reference evidence.

Public sources:
- https://www.target.com/help/articles/policies-guidelines/price-match-guarantee
- https://www.target.com/help/article/000194850

## Best Buy: official API remains a strong launch candidate

Best Buy's public developer portal continues to advertise official Product, Store, and Category APIs. The Products API documentation states that product data includes pricing and availability and that most product information, including pricing, is updated near real-time.

Engineering implications:

1. Best Buy remains a high-priority official connector because it has a documented public developer surface rather than requiring reverse engineering.
2. HUNTIQ should still timestamp every observation and apply freshness confidence; `near real-time` is not the same as guaranteed instantaneous inventory/price state.
3. Production integration still requires a Best Buy API key and acceptance of its developer terms before live ingestion.

Public sources:
- https://developer.bestbuy.com/
- https://developer.bestbuy.com/apis

## Resale comp freshness policy shipped in 0.9.3

HUNTIQ now time-decays resale evidence rather than treating a 180-day-old sold comp as equivalent to a sale from last week. Verified sold comps use a longer half-life than active asking evidence, and 90+ day sold comps are explicitly tracked as stale. Alerts can require both a minimum count of fresh sold comps and a minimum aggregate comp-freshness score.

This complements the existing product-identity, sold-vs-asking, sell-through, stress, and capital-velocity guardrails and reduces false-positive profit estimates in fast-moving categories.
