Downside and risk control

Pretty discounts can still lose money

Fees and friction first

Marketplace percentage fees, shipping, tax, and holding cost are shown before profit and ROI. Percentage fees follow the venue fee base, including buyer-paid shipping or collected tax when the marketplace charges fees on those amounts.

Conservative exits

HUNTIQ stress-tests liquidation value, later-unit decay, and marketplace cost drift so a six-unit promotion cannot inherit the confidence of a one-unit flip.

Conflicts fail closed

Conflicting retailer prices, thin history, mismatched products, or missing sold evidence reduce confidence. They are not averaged into a high-confidence buy.

Demo cannot alert

Demonstration, cached, delayed, and validation-only rows stay ineligible for notifications. Only fresh, validated live observations can retain alert eligibility after the existing safety floor.

How HUNTIQ scores

Evidence first, hype second.

Every opportunity combines store-local price-history deviation, history stability and sample strength, source freshness and provenance reliability, cross-source price consensus, multi-source confirmation, product-match confidence, markdown velocity, resale confidence, comp freshness, liquidity, estimated fees, liquidation value, shipping, profit and ROI. Marketplace percentage fees are calculated against the venue-specific fee base, including buyer-paid shipping or collected sales tax when the marketplace charges fees on those amounts, so card-level profit/ROI and alert eligibility do not inherit item-price-only economics. Required purchase quantity is checked against recent sold-market depth, and HUNTIQ models how much acquisition capital is still tied up after 30, 60 and 90 days so a six-unit promotion cannot inherit the confidence of a one-unit flip. Later units are stress-tested against weakening resale prices, marketplace-specific fee/shipping/holding-cost drift, and an additional cost-uncertainty buffer when exact future selling costs are not known. Future-dated retailer observations are rejected from price-history evidence so a bad clock or malformed feed cannot manufacture sample strength or freshness. Duplicate price-history timestamps are collapsed before anomaly scoring. Thin or volatile histories, conflicting store prices, weak source provenance, mismatched products, stale resale evidence, or unconfirmed promotion quantities are downgraded before they can inflate an alert.

Resale intelligence

Sold data stays sold data.

HUNTIQ separates Verified Sold, Current Asking, and Estimated Market Value, time-decays older comparable sales, discounts low-quality comparable evidence, then stress-tests a conservative liquidation value, confidence-adjusted resale value, break-even resale price, maximum safe buy price, and the best configured resale exit before an alert qualifies. Multi-unit deals show expected units sold, realized profit and capital still tied up at 30/60/90 days. Falling sold-price trends reduce later-unit exit value, while marketplace stress can increase later fees, shipping and holding costs and reserve an additional uncertainty buffer for variable marketplace costs before those units reach the decision floor. Future rebates or store credits are valued separately from cash paid at checkout, basket-level rewards are allocated across qualifying items, and buy-X-get-Y or quantity-tier savings only enter ROI after the full qualifying quantity is confirmed.
